📖 Saving vs. Investing

Two Different Tools

Saving keeps money safe for short-term needs (emergency fund, upcoming purchases). Investing puts money to work for long-term growth — typically earning much higher returns than a savings account, but with more risk.

🏦
Savings Account
~1-5% annual return. Very safe, FDIC insured. Best for money you might need soon.
📈
Stock Market
~7-10% average annual return historically. Higher risk, higher reward. Best for money you won't need for 5+ years.

🧩 Types of Investments

🏢
Stocks
Tiny ownership shares in companies. When the company does well, your stock goes up. Higher risk, higher potential reward. Think Apple, Amazon, Nike.
📜
Bonds
Loans to governments or companies. More stable than stocks but lower returns. Like being the lender instead of the borrower.
🧺
Index Funds / ETFs
Bundles of many stocks in one investment. Spreading risk across hundreds of companies. Experts recommend these for beginners — low cost, diversified!
🏘️
Real Estate
Owning property that earns rent or appreciates in value. High upfront cost but historically strong long-term returns.

⚖️ Risk vs. Reward

⚠️ No Such Thing as "Safe" High Returns

Higher potential returns always come with higher risk. Anyone promising guaranteed 20%+ returns is likely a scam. Diversify your investments to manage risk — never put all your money in one place.

💡 Time Is Your Greatest Advantage

A 15-year-old who invests $1,000 will, on average, end up with FAR more at age 65 than a 35-year-old who invests $10,000 — because of 20 extra years of compound growth. Start as early as you can, even with small amounts!

🚀 How Teens Can Start Investing

1
Ask a parent to open a custodial brokerage account (Fidelity, Schwab, or similar) in your name.
2
Start with a total market index fund (like VTSAX or VTI) — simple, diversified, low-cost.
3
Invest regularly — even $5 or $10 a month adds up enormously over decades.
4
Don't panic sell during market dips — the market always recovers historically.
