📖 The Story of Money

Before Money: The Barter System

Long ago, people traded goods directly — a farmer might swap wheat for a blacksmith's tools. This is called bartering. The problem? You had to find someone who had exactly what you needed AND wanted what you had. Imagine trying to trade 50 apples for a pair of shoes!

Money solved this problem by becoming a universal medium of exchange. Instead of trading goods directly, people could earn money for their work or goods and use that money to buy anything they needed.

Commodity Money

Early "money" was items with real value — gold, silver, salt, shells, and even cattle. These were valuable in themselves. The word "salary" actually comes from "sal" (Latin for salt) because Roman soldiers were sometimes paid in salt!

Coins and Paper Money

Around 600 BC in Lydia (modern Turkey), the first metal coins were minted. Paper money started in China around 600 AD. Paper itself has no real value — it works because everyone agrees it does. This is called fiat money.

⚙️ The 3 Functions of Money

1
Medium of Exchange
Money is accepted by everyone in trade, so you can buy anything without complex bartering.
2
Store of Value
Money holds its value over time — you can save $20 today and spend it next month.
3
Unit of Account
Money gives us a common way to measure and compare the value of things.

💡 Key Vocabulary

💱
Barter
Trading goods or services directly without using money.
💵
Fiat Money
Money that has value because a government says it does, not because of what it's made of.
💳
Digital Money
Money that exists electronically — like what's in a bank account or paid via an app.
🌍
Currency
The specific form of money used in a country (dollars, euros, yen, etc.).

💻 Money Today: Digital & Beyond

Today, most money never exists as physical cash. When your parents pay bills online, transfer funds, or tap a phone to pay — that's digital money moving between accounts. Some newer forms include:

💡 Did You Know?

Less than 8% of all money in the world exists as physical cash. The rest is just numbers in computer systems at banks!

📱
Mobile Payments: Apple Pay, Google Pay, Venmo — tap your phone to pay.
🌐
Cryptocurrency: Digital currencies like Bitcoin, secured by math (not governments).
🏦
Bank Transfers: Moving money electronically between accounts instantly.

🎮 Activity: Barter Challenge!

🔄 What Would You Trade?

Imagine you have 10 apples. What would you try to trade them for, and why might that trade be hard without money?

Great thinking! 🌟 You've identified a real challenge with barter — finding someone who has exactly what you want AND wants what you have (called the "double coincidence of wants"). This is exactly why money was invented — to make trade easier for everyone!
