📖 What Is Credit?

Borrowing Now, Paying Later

Credit is the ability to borrow money with a promise to pay it back — usually with extra money called interest. Credit can be helpful (buying a house) or harmful (buying things you can't afford). The key is using it wisely.

⚡ How Interest Works Against You

⚠️ The Interest Trap

Credit cards charge very high interest rates — often 20-25% per year. If you charge $100 and only make minimum payments, you could end up paying $200+ over time! Never carry a credit card balance if you can avoid it.

The Golden Rule of Credit Cards

Only charge what you can pay off in FULL each month. Treat a credit card like a debit card — only spend money you already have. This way you get the benefits (rewards, fraud protection) without the interest charges.

📊 Your Credit Score: Why It Matters

A credit score (300–850) is a number that tells lenders how trustworthy you are with money. A higher score = lower interest rates = thousands saved over your lifetime.

💚
750–850: Excellent
Best interest rates, easy loan approvals. This is the goal!
💛
670–749: Good
Solid score, decent rates. Most people with good habits land here.
🟠
580–669: Fair
Higher interest rates. Time to improve your habits.
🔴
Below 580: Poor
Difficulty getting loans. Serious work needed to rebuild.

🌟 Good Debt vs. Bad Debt

✅
Good Debt
Borrowing to invest in your future — student loans for a degree that leads to a higher-paying career, a mortgage on a home that builds equity. Still use caution!
❌
Bad Debt
High-interest debt for things that lose value — credit card balances, payday loans, financing luxury items. Avoid at all costs!

🛡️ How to Build Good Credit Young

1
Pay every bill on time — payment history is 35% of your score.
2
Keep credit card balances low (under 30% of your limit).
3
Don't open too many accounts at once.
4
Ask to be added as an authorized user on a parent's card to start building credit.
